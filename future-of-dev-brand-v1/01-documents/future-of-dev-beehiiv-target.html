Your output tripled.
Your review capacity didn't.

The bottleneck in digital work moved one step downstream, into a role nobody has been trained for.

The Shift 01

Three years ago the bottleneck in most digital work was producing the first version — of the analysis, the campaign, the spec, the code. For a lot of teams that bottleneck has quietly gone. What replaced it is less discussed and considerably more awkward.

Somebody still has to decide whether the output is any good. That person is now working through three or four times the material with exactly the same amount of attention they had before.

The pattern is the same everywhere you look. A marketing team ships more variants than anyone can meaningfully test. An analyst produces models faster than the business can interrogate them. An engineer opens changes faster than a reviewer can hold the whole thing in their head. None of the work disappeared. It moved.

Why it Matters 02

The scarce skill is no longer producing a competent draft. It is being able to say precisely why something is wrong, quickly, and with enough authority that the team acts on it.

That is judgement, and judgement is built by having done the work rather than by having watched it be done. Which cuts two ways. If you are already in a role, the accumulated sense of what "wrong" looks like that you have been carrying around for years just became the scarce input. If you are entering the industry, the junior tasks that used to build that sense are the ones being automated first — so you will have to build it deliberately instead of picking it up by accident.

Learn 03

Write the rejection criteria before you look at the output

Reviewing is a search problem. If you open a document, a dashboard or a diff without knowing what you are searching for, you will find whatever is most visually salient — an odd phrase, a badly named variable, a chart with the wrong colours — and you will miss the thing that actually matters.

So before you look, write down two things: the one failure that would make this unusable, and the one failure you would be most likely to miss. Two lines. It takes about ninety seconds and it changes what your eyes do.

Then run three passes, in this order. Most reviewers only ever run the first.

CorrectnessIs it true? Do the numbers, the claims and the logic hold up on their own terms?
FitIs it the right thing for this audience, system or moment — not just a good thing in general?
ConsequenceWhat breaks in three months if this is wrong, and who finds out first?

AI output is disproportionately strong on the first pass and disproportionately weak on the third, because consequence depends on context the model was never given: your customers, your last outage, the promise someone made in a meeting it wasn't in. That asymmetry is the whole game.

None of this is new, and that is rather the point. It is how good editors, reviewers and analysts have always worked. What changed is that it used to be a small part of the job and is now most of it.

Build 04

Audit the last three things you accepted

Find three outputs you accepted from an AI tool this week — a draft, a query, a summary, a block of code. For each one, write the rejection criteria you should have written first.

Then mark the ones you could genuinely have written in advance. The ones you couldn't are the tasks you are delegating without fully understanding them. That list is your reading list.

TypeHelvetica, not Archivo

The Post Builder can't take custom fonts. The issue still reads as Future of Dev because the layout, the mono labels, the 2px ink rule under the masthead and the lime block do the work — not the typeface.

StructureNumbered blocks, every week

Each block carries its icon, its name and its position. By issue three the reader can skip to Build without reading anything else, which is the behaviour you want to measure.

ColourLime appears exactly once

One block per issue. That scarcity is what makes it mean "do something" rather than "we like green". If a second lime element appears, one of them is wrong.

DeliverabilityOne CTA per block, max

Keep, Go Deeper and the footer are the only links that matter. Resist adding a sponsor slot until the habit is established — the brief gates that behind audience scale.

MeasurementBuild is the north-star event

Weekly Activated Readers depends on someone doing the exercise. The Keep download and the Build block are the two clicks worth instrumenting properly.

Non-negotiableNo image-only headlines

Tempting, since images would let you use Archivo. Don't — they break in dark mode, fail for screen readers and get stripped by corporate clients. Type stays as live text.

Part C / Build sheet

Where each decision
lives in Beehiiv

The gap between a mock and a shipped publication is usually a list of fields nobody wrote down. This is that list, mapped to Beehiiv's actual named settings.

Typography — two systems, not one

This is the single most important constraint. Beehiiv's Website Builder accepts Google Fonts and custom uploads; the Post Builder is restricted to a curated email-safe set and does not accept custom fonts at all — including in welcome and automation emails.

Colour — mapped to Beehiiv's named slots

Beehiiv's Style panel doesn't use role names like "ink" or "signal". It uses six fixed slots, and one of them behaves awkwardly. Set these, then check the awkward one in preview before you trust it.

Beehiiv slotSet toWhat it drives
Outside Background#F3F5F8 CloudThe canvas behind the email body
Post Background#FFFFFF BaseThe reading surface
Text on Background#111827 InkAll body copy
Primary#111827 InkFooter background, buttons, blockquotes, table cells, byline
Text on Primary#FFFFFF — verifyFooter text and the post title. Because it colours both, a dark Primary can leave your title white on white. If preview shows that, set Primary to Cloud #F3F5F8 and Text on Primary to Ink instead, and build the dark footer as its own block.
Secondary#E2E6EC HairlineDividers, footer top border, blockquote borders
Link colour#4355FF SignalInline links. 5.27:1 on white — passes AA.

Build lime is not in that list on purpose. None of Beehiiv's six slots corresponds to an accent surface, so the Build block has to be built as a component — a styled blockquote or a code/HTML block with a background-filled table cell — and saved into the template once. Never set Primary to lime: it would turn every button and the whole footer green, and lime measures 1.18:1 against white.

Build order

Fonts on the site first

Website Builder → Settings → Themes → Custom fonts. Upload Archivo, Inter and JetBrains Mono as WOFF2, or select them from the Google Fonts library. Assign to Header / Body / Button in Edit Theme.

Export the masthead PNG

Two versions at 2× — ink-on-white for light, white-and-lime for dark — plus a favicon at 32 and 16. The 16px symbol-only version is the floor.

Set the Post Builder style once

Colours and the H1–H6 scale in the Style panel. Then save it as a template post named FoD Weekly v1 so the styling never has to be re-applied.

Put the six blocks into the template as placeholders

The Shift / Why it Matters / Learn / Build / Keep / Go Deeper, each with its label, icon and number already in place. Drafting becomes filling boxes, which is what protects the weekly cadence.

Mirror the colours on the web Post page

Background, text and link colours on the web version of a post are controlled by the Website Builder, not the Post Builder. Set them to match or your archive will drift from your inbox.

Wire the welcome survey

Career stage, work area, what they want AI to help with, confidence level, and what would make this valuable in 30 days. Stage becomes the segment everything else keys off.

Verify on the first test send

Gmail dark mode: masthead still legible, Build block still lime, title not inverted into invisibility.
Outlook desktop: falls back to Arial without the layout collapsing. Block labels still readable.
Images off: the alt text on the masthead reads "Future of Dev", and no headline has disappeared.
Phone at 375px: single column intact, Build block padding not crushed, buttons still tappable.
Web version of the post matches the email — same background, link colour and type scale.
Lime appears exactly once in the whole email. If it appears twice, one of them is decoration.

Beehiiv's builders change often, and custom-CSS support in particular has moved more than once. Treat the field names above as current-as-of-now and confirm them in the app before handing this to anyone.
