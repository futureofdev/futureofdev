# Relaunch strategy

## Strategic position

Future of Dev is the newsletter-led learning brand for people evolving with AI
and entering the industry. It helps readers understand what is changing,
develop useful skills and build something real, one edition at a time.

The short position is:

> Learn and build the AI-native way.

The newsletter is the product. The website is the public archive and conversion
layer. Courses, resources and future paid offers deepen the relationship; they
do not compete with the newsletter for brand primacy.

## Audience order

### 1. Evolve

Professionals already in digital work who need to adapt their craft, judgement
and operating model as AI changes how work is done. This is the launch audience
and should lead homepage examples, newsletter framing and founder distribution.

### 2. Enter

Students, graduates, career changers and early-career practitioners who need
relevant skills and credible evidence of capability. This is the growth
audience. Coding Bootcamp in a Box is its separate entry on-ramp.

### 3. Lead

Managers, educators and senior practitioners shaping standards, teams and
learning systems. This is an expansion audience, not the primary launch lens.

The audiences share one need: agency in a changing field. Segmentation changes
examples and recommendations, not the core brand.

## Brand architecture

Future of Dev remains one master brand under `futureofdev.com`.

- Newsletter editions interpret a shift and create a practical action.
- Insights are the canonical public archive of free editions.
- Courses provide structured, applied learning.
- Skill-based learning is the method underneath every course, not a separate
  product or navigation category.
- Resources are extracted from proven issues and courses, not created as
  disconnected lead magnets.

## Editorial promise

Every edition should help a reader do three things:

1. Understand one meaningful change.
2. Learn a durable method or principle.
3. Build or check something in a realistic context.

The standard issue anatomy is:

| Block | Reader value |
| --- | --- |
| The Shift | One important change in tools, work or expectations |
| Why It Matters | The consequence for career, craft or team |
| Learn | One principle, workflow or decision framework |
| Build | A focused 15 to 30 minute exercise |
| Keep | A reusable prompt, checklist, template or reference |
| Go Deeper | One relevant course, resource or prior edition |

Go Deeper has one call to action. On course release weeks, that call to action
is the course and the Build block is a small, honest taste of its work.

### Editorial mix

| Pillar | Guide share | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| AI-native workflows | 30% | Show how work is changing in practice |
| Skills and craft | 25% | Teach durable capability |
| Career evolution and entry | 20% | Address relevance, employability and progression |
| Tools in context | 15% | Explain useful products without becoming a news feed |
| Builders and teams | 10% | Create proof, humanity and community |

The percentages guide portfolio review; they are not quotas for an individual
month.

## Publishing model

The relaunch cadence is weekly and intentionally weekday-neutral until an
operator selects a date after the readiness gate passes.

Before public relaunch:

- Bank four complete issues, including every Build and Keep asset.
- Test the Beehiiv template and sending setup across the clients in the
  runbook.
- Publish and pin the supported Skilling CLI release, then pass the bootcamp's
  clean-download onboarding and delivery checks through that CLI.
- Prepare one founder-led LinkedIn post for each issue.
- Give each LinkedIn link explicit `utm_source=linkedin`,
  `utm_medium=social` and an issue-specific `utm_campaign` value.

After relaunch, monthly deep dives and quarterly state-of-the-field editions can
be introduced only if the weekly habit remains stable. A missed complex issue
becomes a smaller field note; it does not create a silent week.

## Learning strategy

Courses use a capability-first loop:

1. Understand: establish the useful mental model.
2. Learn: introduce a repeatable method.
3. Build: apply it in a realistic context.
4. Demonstrate: test the result against visible criteria.
5. Reflect: improve the judgement used next time.

Every course must be able to complete this statement:

> You will develop [skill] by building [artefact] in [realistic context], and
> demonstrate it against [quality criteria].

Course creation is gated by evidence:

1. Publish at least one strong issue on the topic.
2. Review clicks, replies, saves and build participation.
3. Interview readers across Evolve and Enter where the decision warrants it.
4. Test a waitlist with a specific outcome and expected effort.
5. Build the smallest course that creates a demonstrable result.
6. Review completion proxies, outcomes and repeated demand before expanding.

Two AI-native catalogue courses must pass this process before a recurring
catalogue release cadence is announced. Coding Bootcamp in a Box remains a
separate Enter product and does not count towards the gate.

## Distribution

Channel priority is:

1. LinkedIn for founder-led interpretation and professional reach.
2. Newsletter referrals and recommendations for compounding acquisition.
3. GitHub for open artefacts, technical credibility and proof of building.
4. Search and the public archive for evergreen discovery.
5. Short video for accessible explanations when the format earns the effort.
6. Partnerships with universities, bootcamps, communities and aligned
   publications after the core habit is stable.

Each newsletter release requires one LinkedIn post. The post should stand on
its own, expose a real artefact or useful idea from the issue, and link to the
canonical insight URL with the required UTM fields. Do not manufacture a thread
of derivative posts solely to fill a calendar.

## Business model

Free value must create real progress. Paid offers sell depth, structure,
feedback, convenience or team application, not withheld fundamentals.

The sequence is:

1. Free weekly newsletter for habit, trust and owned audience.
2. Free templates, guides and short learning experiences for activation and
   segmentation.
3. Paid practical guides or courses after demand is demonstrated.
4. Recurring premium depth only after repeated retention evidence.
5. Team programmes, sponsorship or partnerships only after audience and
   editorial trust support them.

Do not promise that every future product will be free. Promise meaningful free
access. Sponsorship must never determine editorial judgement.

## Experience principles

The brand should feel like a modern editorial publication with the energy of a
product studio.

- Lead with the editorial masthead, not an education dashboard.
- Use real artefacts, annotations and working examples before illustration.
- Use structured grids, modular learning blocks and generous whitespace.
- Use Build lime to signal action, once per layout.
- Pair every career-stage colour with an icon and a word.
- Avoid generic AI futurism: robots, glowing brains, unreadable code walls and
  purple-neon gradients.
- Keep the newsletter conversion path ahead of course browsing.

The homepage order should prove the promise quickly: hero and signup, what each
edition gives the reader, latest edition, featured learning, audience, founder
credibility, mission, real reader proof when it exists, and a final specific
signup.

## Risks and controls

| Risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| The audience becomes generic | Lead with Evolve, segment examples, keep one shared need |
| Fear replaces agency | Every disruption claim must lead to a practical action |
| Courses disrupt publishing | Keep the newsletter gate and demand-led course gate |
| Beehiiv is treated as an LMS | Use email-led learning until learner needs justify another system |
| Tool coverage dates quickly | Teach transferable primitives and mark reviewed dates |
| Free positioning blocks revenue | Promise useful free access, not universal free products |
| Founder dependency becomes brittle | Maintain voice, issue structure and operating skills in the repository |

## Strategic test

Ask one question before adding a page, issue, course or offer:

> Does this help someone understand the change, develop a useful skill and
> build something real in their career or work?

If it only reports what happened or makes the brand look futuristic, it is not
enough.
